# Frame: a clearer game, then a longer one

Status: **approved 2026-11-28** ("Go with recommendations"), except decision 3: tending the young stays (section 2.4). Phase 1 is being built.

The author, with his fifth playtest save (2026-11-28):

> "I'm thinking there is getting to be too much writing in the screens at one
> time, not enough icons, animation, or art, everything feels a bit scrunched
> up. And we could grow the game to go until the end of the dinosaurs, with
> more evolution. I feel like having all of the different mouth adaptations
> didn't make much sense. There is a lot to keep track of right now and it can
> be easy to forget the rivals, ways of living, and body pressures all the
> time"

Four asks: fewer words and more picture; something to keep track of the
standing orders; mouths that make sense; and a game that runs to the end of
the dinosaurs. The recommendation is to do them in that order. Adding eras
and traits to a game that is already hard to follow would make the
"too much to keep track of" problem worse.

## 0. What the fifth run shows

One life, the Triassic's last die-off came cold. **Came through, second of
eight** (standing 607), a body most like the crocodile line.

- **18 traits at the end.** The mouth went from fish to hunter in the Coal
  Forests, then picked up a scavenger's habit, crushing teeth and an
  omnivore's teeth: three refinements pulling three ways off one jaw.
- **Every era was a boom and a crash.** From each era's peak to its end:
  4,800 to 1,600 heads (the Shore's cold), 18,200 to 6,800 (the Coal
  Forests), 20,000 to 1,000 (the Drying's heat), 25,600 to 370 (the Great
  Dying, the closest the run came to ending) and 20,100 to 2,200 (the
  Triassic's cold).
- **No stance toward a rival at the end, and rivals left: none.** The save
  does not record stances turn by turn, so it cannot say how often rivals
  were dealt with. That fits the author's own note that they are easy to
  forget.

## 1. Why it is hard to keep track of

Measured on today's screens, mid-run in the Coal Forests, on a phone 390 by
844 points (one turn of a crocodile-line run, header and tabs counted):

| Screen | Height | Words on it |
|---|---|---|
| Turn | 1.7 phone screens | 213 |
| Body | 3.8 phone screens | 501 |
| Rivals | 1.2 phone screens | 228 |

Three standing orders steer the run: what you **press** (a trait growing),
how you **live** (ways of living) and your **stance** toward a rival. They
sit on three different screens. None of them asks for attention when it
should. A rival can start hunting you, a die-off can be named, or a pressure
can finish, and the only sign is a line of text among 200 others.

Two of the four stances, and one way of living, are worth almost nothing.
They are one more thing to read every turn for no return (round D gate,
1,000 seeds an arm, against none):

| Choice | Best on any body | Floor |
|---|---|---|
| Keep out of their way | +0.1 | 4.1 |
| Follow a bigger hunter | +1.0 (mammal) | 4.1 |
| Tending the young | +1.6 (careful) | 4.1 |

## 2. Phase 1: clarity (no new content)

**2.1 Three slots, always on the Turn screen.** One row under the picture:
*Growing* (the trait being pressed, with a ring that fills), *Living* (the
ways' icons) and *Rivals* (the stance's icon). Tap one to open its sheet.
A slot is marked when it needs a look:

- Growing: nothing is being pressed, or what was pressed is done.
- Living: a die-off has been named (laying down fat is the answer to that).
- Rivals: a rival is hunting or pushing you and you have no stance.

The mark says *look*; it never says what to do.

**2.2 A word budget.** The Turn screen shows at most 60 words before a tap
(today 213). Every line of explanation moves into the sheet behind its
icon. The phone smoke test counts the words and fails over the budget, so it
stays true.

**2.3 Offers as cards.** A trait on offer is an icon, its name and three
marks: food (up or down), how many are caught (up or down), upkeep. The
sentence that explains it is in the sheet. Today each offer is a paragraph.

**2.4 Fewer choices that do nothing.**
- Stances go from four to two: *push* and *prey*, or none. Following a
  hunter becomes part of the scavenger's mouth (a scavenger eats behind
  hunters on its ground without a stance).
- Tending the young was to be cut as a way of living, on a measurement of
  +1.6 points at best. **It stays.** That number was tending against no way
  of living at all. Cut from play, it cost the crocodile 20 points and the
  turtle 12 (200 seeds a body); made something every lineage does, it made
  every rival breed faster and cost as much.

**2.5 Mouths that make sense.** Today any jaw can grow any refinement. The
fifth run's hunter grew crushing teeth and an omnivore's teeth. Proposed:
each refinement belongs to the jaw it makes sense on, and the tree draws it
under that jaw.

| Jaw | Its refinement |
|---|---|
| A hunter's jaw | A scavenger: eats the dead, and what bigger hunters leave |
| A grazer's jaw | Cracking and grinding: seeds, hard plants |
| A fisher's jaw | Shell-crushing teeth: shellfish |
| An insect-eater's jaw | A long tongue: insects other mouths cannot reach |
| Any jaw | An omnivore's teeth: some of every other food. The one general road. (It was to cost more; at 0.05 it cost careful play 3 points and stays 0.04) |

The long tongue is new. The other four already exist and are re-homed.

## 3. Phase 2: the look

- **The picture grows** to about half the screen, and becomes the run at a
  glance. Your herd is drawn as several animals, more or fewer as numbers
  rise and fall. Rivals are drawn beside you, turned toward you when they
  hunt or push. Every die-off has its weather (snow, heat shimmer, ash).
- **Icons for everything** a player chooses: every trait, food, way of
  living, stance, region and die-off. Pixel icons in the sprite's own style,
  drawn in code like the animal, so each one comes from its data and none
  is a file to keep in step. About 50 in all.
- **Movement.** Numbers count up and down between turns; a trait that grows
  flashes on the animal; a die-off rolls in over its warning turns. All of
  it respects the phone's reduce-motion setting.
- **Room.** Fewer boxed panels; spacing on an 8 px grid; one idea per block.

## 4. Phase 3: to the end of the dinosaurs

The run ends today at the end of the Triassic (about 201 million years ago).
Proposed: three more eras, ending at the end of the Cretaceous (about 66
million years ago). The dates and die-offs below are from memory and are
checked before they shape a number.

| Era | About | Its die-off(s) |
|---|---|---|
| The Jurassic | 201 to 145 Ma | A warming that starves the seas of air (about 183 Ma) |
| The Early Cretaceous | 145 to 100 Ma | Flowering plants arrive: plants and insects grow, a change of ground, not only a die-off |
| The Late Cretaceous | 100 to 66 Ma | The impact, and the burning of the Deccan: the last die-off, as the Great Dying is today |

**More evolution**, each trait built over several turns and resting on
earlier ones, as now:

- **Flight**, by feathers then hollow bones then wings: easy moves between
  regions, fewer caught. It closes off armour and a heavier body.
- **A long neck**, resting on a heavier body: reaches plants no one else can.
- **Teeth in batteries**, resting on grinding: the best plant-eater's mouth.
- **Breathing through air sacs**, resting on lungs at step 3: better in heat
  and in thin air.
- **Milk**, resting on fur and warm blood: the young come through a lean
  year.
- **Back to the water**: flippers. It closes off legs under the body.

**The ladder at the end** becomes the lineages that came through the
impact: birds, mammals, crocodiles, turtles, lizards and snakes, frogs and
salamanders, and one more, all scored by the same function as today.

**The one real risk: history says big bodies died.** The impact killed
nearly everything over about 25 kg. The game's rule is that any body can
come through ("I don't think winning should be reliant on fitting a list of
traits"). Both hold if the warning gives a big lineage a real road: shrink,
go to ground, or live off the dead and the rivers, as the survivors did. A
big body that sees the warning and changes is a road. One that cannot
change in time is a gate, and the gate will measure which it is.

**Length.** The run grows from 107 turns to about 160, or the eras get
shorter. The kill gate takes about 1.5 times as long; it has to be split
across the machine's cores to finish inside the two-hour limit.

### 4.1 Phase 3 in detail (written 2026-11-28, before building)

The history behind it is in `docs/HISTORY.md` (searched, not read in the
papers). One change from the table above: the Cenomanian-Turonian event
(93.9 Ma) is inside the Late Cretaceous, so the era of flowers ends on it.

| Era | Span | Turns | In the era | Its die-off, by face |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Jurassic | 201 to 145 Ma | 18 | Seas starved of air near 183 Ma: a few turns of warmth and fewer fish | The end of the Jurassic: the seas fall back, and big bodies are hit hardest (a **heavy** face), or a drought |
| The Age of Flowers | 145 to 94 Ma | 18 | Flowering plants spread: more plants and insects for the rest of the era | The dead seas: a sharp warming and few fish, or a heat |
| The Last of the Giants | 94 to 66 Ma | 22 | | **The impact**, named 8 turns out as the Great Dying is: a dark winter, a burning, or the Deccan's long poison. Each falls hardest on big bodies |

The run grows from 107 turns to 165.

**A die-off that falls on size.** Today a die-off takes food and moves heat
and wet; a heavy body only does worse through what it eats. Two of the new
faces also take a share of a lineage for each step of size above the first
(`big` on the die-off). That is how the history reads (most land animals
over about 25 kg died at the impact), and it is a warning a player can act
on: size can be let go a step at a time (`SHED_TURNS`), and eight turns of
warning are enough for two steps.

**New traits arrive with their era.** A trait may carry `from`, the first
era it can turn up in. The tree shows it from the start with "from the
Jurassic"; it cannot be watched for or offered before. Six traits, each
built over several turns and resting on earlier ones:

| Trait | Rests on | Does | Closes off |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wings (gliding, then flight) | Feathers | Much quicker to move and harder to catch | A heavier body at step 2; armour |
| A long neck | A heavier body | Reaches plants no one else can | Burrowing |
| Teeth in batteries | A grazer's jaw at step 3 | The best plant-eater's mouth (a refinement of that jaw) | |
| Air sacs | Lungs at step 3 | Better in heat and in the dead seas' thin air | |
| Milk | Fur and warm blood | The young come through a lean year: numbers fall more slowly | |
| Flippers | | Fish feed you far better | Legs under the body at step 2 |

**The ladder at the end** is the lineages that came through the impact,
scored on what came after (the first forests of the new age): birds,
mammals, crocodiles, turtles, lizards and snakes, frogs and salamanders, and
the champsosaurs (fish-eaters of the rivers, which also came through). The
dinosaur and beaked plant-eater bodies leave it. A big dinosaur body can
still come through by changing in time; the gate measures it with its own
bot (a big body that heeds the warning and sheds size).

**Saves.** Version 8. A run that has ended stays ended; a run still going
walks on past the Triassic into the Jurassic.

**Order of the build.** (1) The eras, their die-offs and the ladder, with
the bots taught the new die-offs; measured. (2) The six traits; measured.
(3) Tuned to the targets on the whole run; the full gate.

## 6. Decisions for the author

| # | Question | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Order: clarity, then the look, then the longer game? | Yes. Each era and trait added to today's screens makes the forgetting worse |
| 2 | Stances down to push and prey; following a hunter folded into the scavenger's mouth? | Yes. Avoid and follow are worth 0.1 and 1.0 points at best |
